About

Lara Solomon is an Australian healthcare business owner and operations manager based in Sydney, New South Wales. She owns and manages Rose Bay Medical Clinic, a general practice in Rose Bay in Sydney's eastern suburbs, and runs operations and customer management for Redrew Clothing, a sustainable womenswear label designed and made in Sydney.

Outside of business, she works in enumerative combinatorics. In July 2026 the On-Line Encyclopedia of Integer Sequences credited her with the thirteenth terms of two sequences counting self-avoiding paths on square grid graphs — extending records first published in 2014 and 2017, on a problem the encyclopedia classifies as computationally hard.

Two very different disciplines, one habit: taking a system that nobody has fully mapped and working out exactly how it behaves.

Published Mathematics

On-Line Encyclopedia of Integer Sequences

Contributor — three sequences

The OEIS is the reference database of integer sequences, founded in 1964 by Neil Sloane and cited across mathematics and computer science. Lara's contributions all concern the same stubborn problem: counting self-avoiding paths on a square grid graph — every route through an n × n lattice that never revisits a point. The count explodes so violently with n that each additional term is its own computational project.

  1. A236753 Number of simple (non-intersecting) directed paths on the grid graph Pn × Pn a(13) — 30 July 2026

    Flagged hard by the OEIS. Lara computed the 13 × 13 case — a 39-digit number:

    779 903 870 849 450 440 101 587 633 989 096 314 113
  2. A288032 Number of (undirected) paths in the n × n grid graph a(13) — 30 July 2026

    The undirected companion result, contributed the same day. The two values are self-checking: the directed count is exactly twice the undirected count plus the 169 single-vertex paths of a 13 × 13 grid.

    389 951 935 424 725 220 050 793 816 994 548 156 972
  3. A288518 Array read by antidiagonals: T(m,n) = number of (undirected) paths in the grid graph Pm × Pn b-file extended to 351 terms

    The general rectangular case. Lara extended the sequence's data file from 276 terms to 351 — three complete new antidiagonals added to a table whose previous 276 terms were the work of Andrew Howroyd.
